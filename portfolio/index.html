THE PURPOSE

A study in visual communication.

Welcome to this graphics design portfolio. Graphic design combines typography, imagery, colour and layout to communicate ideas to an audience. Effective design considers both how something looks and how clearly its message can be understood.

This portfolio connects design theory with visual demonstrations and practical coursework. Its purpose is to explain key concepts, explore responsible use of creative work, and show how design decisions support communication.

A design process: from brief to finished work

  1. Understand the brief. Identify the audience, purpose, message and required output. A poster announcing an event has different priorities from a small logo.
  2. Research responsibly. Collect references and record the creator, licence and source of any assets. Inspiration should guide a new solution rather than become an uncredited copy.
  3. Explore ideas. Sketch more than one composition. Consider focal points, alignment, colour and the relationship between text and image.
  4. Develop the artwork. Choose tools and formats suited to the work. Use layers, sensible file names and editable source files.
  5. Evaluate and revise. Check accuracy, legibility, visual hierarchy and accessibility. Test the design at its intended size.
  6. Export and reflect. Keep the editable master, export a suitable presentation file and explain what worked and what could improve.

Understanding animation: timing and movement

Animation creates the appearance of change through a sequence of frames or changes over time. Timing controls duration; spacing controls how far an object moves between moments; easing changes the apparent acceleration. Motion should support a message rather than distract from it.

The circle eases between two positions. Pause it to inspect the composition. The demonstration starts paused and respects reduced-motion preferences. This is a generated teaching example, not the student's Working with Animation course artifact.

For the actual artifact, explain the timing, intended message, tools used and export format. Provide a playable file or functioning link, plus a static alternative when useful.
